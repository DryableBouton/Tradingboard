Mindset & pre-market check-in

What this means: Most traders don't lose because of bad setups. They lose because they don't sell: they give back wins and hold losers. Do this 60-second check before 9:30. The ideas are inspired by Mark Douglas's Trading in the Zone (in my words).
Today's principle

You don't need to trade every day

Mark Douglas says the market owes you nothing and offers endless chances. A pro's edge includes the days they choose not to play. Sitting out an unknowable event is a skill, not a missed opportunity.

Applied today: Nothing before 10:00. Only the $768.65 hold triggers a trade. Stop and target 1 go in at fill. 12:00 no trigger = done. Flat by 1:30. Watch the Fed with no position.

Pre-market check-in (tap to tick)
Any "no" or "yes, I'm chasing"? Trade half size or sit today out. Sitting out is a position.
House rules (non-negotiable)
  1. Place the stop as a real order the moment you enter. It's decided before entry, not during.
  2. Never move a stop further away. You can only tighten it.
  3. No averaging down on short-term trades. A loser doesn't get more money.
  4. Take partial profits at target 1 (sell ⅓, or all if under 3 contracts), then stop to breakeven.
  5. Hit your daily loss limit → done for the day. Close the app.
  6. No revenge trades: after a loss, the next trade must be a planned setup from the board.
  7. Only trade setups on today's board, at the size the board calculates.
Every broken rule gets logged your trade journal, even when the trade made money.

Options ideas

What this means: For each idea, here is a menu of real option contracts, from "expires today" to "two months out". I mark the one I'd pick for a beginner. Prices come from the live option chain: live mid = halfway between today's bid and ask; last trade = the last price someone paid (it may be stale, so check your broker).
ITM · ATM · OTM: where the strike sits
ITM
In the money. A call's strike is below the stock price (for a put, above). It costs more but already has real value, moves most like the stock (~60–80¢ per $1), and has the best odds.
ATM
At the money. The strike is about equal to the stock price. A balanced middle: ~50¢ per $1, ~50/50 odds. The most traded and the easiest to get in and out of.
OTM
Out of the money. A call's strike is above the stock price. It's cheap, but the stock has to move a lot, fast. It's often worth $0 at expiry: a lottery ticket.
Delta ≈ how many cents the option moves when the stock moves $1. It's also a rough chance the option finishes in the money. One contract = 100 shares, so a delta of 0.50 ≈ $50 per $1 move.
Daily vs weekly vs monthly: the time tradeoff
COSTTIME DECAYODDS / FORGIVENESS
Daily (0DTE)CheapestBrutal: can lose most of its value in hoursYou must be right now. Pros only.

MonthlyMediumModerateThe idea has weeks to work. Best for beginners.

Time decay = what the option loses each day if the stock doesn't move (shown as "Decay/day"). Break-even = where the stock must be at expiry for you to not lose money.

Lesson 15

Why we're flat before the Fed

A Fed decision is a coin flip you can't study. At 2:00 the statement comes out, and at 2:30 the chair talks. SPY can swing 1% either way in minutes, and option prices jump around wildly. Nobody, not even the pros, knows which way.

Holding a short-dated option through that is gambling, not trading. Your stop can't protect you, because price can jump straight past it. That's why every trade today has a hard 1:30 PM exit.

Option buyers get hit twice: before the event, options are pricey because everyone expects a move. Right after, that extra price drains out (called 'IV crush'), so even a correct guess can lose money.

Hold through the Fed: you don't know the direction, the stop can be skipped, and the option loses its event premium. Flat by 1:30: you keep whatever you made, and you can trade tomorrow with fresh information.

Your homework: At 2:00 and 2:30, just watch SPY with no position. Write down how far it moved in the first 15 minutes. That's the risk you avoided.

Plain-English glossary (tap to open)
ETF A fund that trades like a stock and holds a basket of companies (SPY = 500 big US companies).
Premarket Trading from 4:00 to 9:30 AM ET. Fewer traders, so prices jump around more.
Futures Contracts on an index that trade nearly 24 hours. They hint at how stocks will open.
Yield The interest rate a bond pays. When yields rise fast, stocks (especially growth stocks) often fall.
bp (basis point) One hundredth of a percent. A yield move from 5.22% to 5.25% is +3 bp.
VIX The 'fear gauge', built from option prices. Low (under 20) = calm. High (over 30) = panic.
EMA (8 / 21) A moving average of recent prices that weights the newest days more. Price above both = short-term uptrend.
Break and retest Price breaks through a level, comes back to test it, and holds. That's the confirmation we enter on.
Failed breakdown Price briefly breaks a support, traps sellers, then reclaims the level. Often a sharp bounce setup.
Stop The price where you exit to cap the loss. Decided before you enter.
Target The price where you plan to take profit, usually the next level.
Call option The right to buy a stock at a set price (the strike) by a date (expiry). It profits if the stock rises.
Put option The right to sell at the strike. It profits if the stock falls.
Strike The price written into the option contract.
Expiry The date the option dies. The closer it gets, the faster the option loses value (time decay).
Premium The price of an option, quoted per share. One contract = 100 shares, so a $10 premium costs $1,000.
Daily loss limit If we hit our daily loss limit, we stop trading until tomorrow. No exceptions.
Options expiration (opex) The day a big batch of option contracts expires. The third Friday of the month is the largest one. Prices can stick near popular strike prices, then move fast late in the day.
Weekly candle One bar that sums up the whole week: where it opened Monday, the high, the low, and where it closes Friday at 4:00 PM.
Wick The thin line on a candle showing prices that were reached but not held. A long lower wick means sellers pushed price down and buyers pushed it back up.
Capitulation When the last nervous holders give up and sell all at once, often on huge volume. It can mark a short-term low, but only price action afterwards confirms it.
Support / resistance A price where buyers have stepped in before (support) or sellers have (resistance).
Gap When a stock opens well above (gap up) or below (gap down) its previous close because of overnight news.
Bear trap A dip under support that pulls in sellers, then reverses up quickly, forcing them to buy back. Same idea as a failed breakdown.
Either/or plan Two opposite trade plans written in advance; the first one that triggers is taken and the other is cancelled.
Retest Price comes back to a level it just broke through. If the old ceiling now holds as a floor, the breakout is confirmed.
Risk-off A mood where investors sell riskier things (tech, crypto) and buy safer ones (bonds, gold, defensive stocks).
